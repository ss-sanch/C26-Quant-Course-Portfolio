Algorithmic Trading Building Journal: Preparation & Architecture (Weeks 1 - 7)

10th June 2026: The Slippage Reality CheckFirst proper lecture today, and it was a massive reality check. I’m used to trading my own tiny portfolio where I can just smash the 'buy at market' button without a second thought. It turns out that doing that in a live algorithmic environment will get you absolutely destroyed by slippage. To try and fix this, I’m forcing the bot to strictly trade highly liquid mega-caps (like Apple and Nvidia) so the bid-ask spreads don't eat my account alive.Macro-wise, US CPI data printed 2.6% core inflation today, and Kevin Warsh held rates steady. The market completely shrugged off his hawkish tone and the VIX actually dropped. Because of this, I’ve locked in the order logic for my first model: absolutely no market orders. The bot will only use limit orders with hard stops to cap the downside.

12th June 2026: Avoiding the TrapSpent hours today trying to grasp overfitting. It is dangerously easy to tweak a model until it looks like a money-printing machine on historical data, only for it to completely blow up in the live simulation. Going forward, I’m forcing a strict 70/30 split on the data. Reserving that 30% as a pristine out-of-sample testing set is the only way to keep the bot honest before we hit the live market.

17th June 2026: Garbage In, Garbage OutData is an absolute mess. I thought the Yahoo Finance API would just stream perfectly clean price floats, but it kept throwing random NaN values mid-session. Gemini initially wrote a basic script that just filled the gaps with zeros, which instantly crashed the bot because a stock instantly dropping to $0 triggers a fake liquidation event! I had to rip that out and code a proper linear interpolation cleaner to smooth the missing prices out mathematically. Also had to add a spike filter because random API glitches were hitting my stop-losses for absolutely no reason.

19th June 2026: The Nvidia Split BugAnother massive headache. Nvidia had a 4-for-1 split in the historical data, meaning the raw price dropped 75% overnight. The algorithm panicked and read it as a catastrophic market crash. I’ve now forced the entire pipeline to run strictly on the 'Adjusted Close' price. I also spent ages researching Survivorship Bias. If I just backtest on today’s S&P 500 list, the bot looks like a genius because it completely ignores all the companies that went bankrupt over the last decade. I have to actively include the delisted failures in my universe to get real metrics.

21st June 2026: Portfolio MathsFascinating end to the week looking at Markowitz and Modern Portfolio Theory. The maths behind portfolio variance ($\sigma_p^2$) is elegant, proving exactly how diversifying across un-correlated assets reduces overall risk. Still, calculating the optimal Sharpe ratio highlighted the trap of P-Hacking. It is incredibly tempting to just blindly spam parameters until the backtest curve points straight up. If a metric doesn't have a solid, mechanical reason for being in the code, I am aggressively stripping it out.

25th June 2026: The Trend Following IllusionMoved into core strategies this week. The difference between discretionary trading (which is mostly just me panicking at live charts) and algorithmic rules is massive. We coded a simple Trend Following bot that compares a 50-period to a 200-period Moving Average. Honestly? It sounds brilliant in a textbook, but in a sideways ranging market, the bot just gets chewed up by false breakouts. Same goes for the RSI indicator; fading an aggressively strong trend just because the RSI hits 70 is a fantastic way to zero an account.

28th June 2026: Macro ChaosWhat a chaotic week. The macro noise is deafening. Keir Starmer just resigned, making it the seventh PM since Brexit, so UK domestic uncertainty is violently spiking. I am keeping my algorithms entirely away from the FTSE 250 for now. Meanwhile, globally, we have US-Iran peace talks causing oil volatility and a tech hangover from the SpaceX IPO. The S&P is hovering around 7,500, but smaller semiconductor names are taking over the leadership.

30th June 2026: Pivoting to Stat-ArbBecause the market is so news-driven right now, my basic trend-following bot is completely useless. I’m pivoting to Statistical Arbitrage (Pairs Trading). The maths is built on cointegration. If the price spread between two highly correlated energy majors massively diverges because of an Iranian oil headline, I calculate the Z-score of that spread. If it stretches too wide, the bot shorts the winner and buys the loser, betting that the spread will mean-revert.

2nd July 2026: Lagging IndicatorsBacktesting and strategy evaluation today, which was a real eye-opener. The standard Simple Moving Average crossover we built last week is painfully slow. By the time the 50-day SMA actually crosses the 200-day, the trend is practically over and I'm buying the absolute top. I did some independent reading and found Kaufman’s Adaptive Moving Average (KAMA). It mathematically accounts for market volatility, slowing down during the chop to avoid fake signals and speeding up when a real trend hits.

4th July 2026: Slippage is Bleeding Me DryThe political vacuum in the UK has turned the FTSE 100 into a schizophrenic mess. The volatility today perfectly highlighted why backtests lie. Most retail traders assume they’ll get filled exactly at the closing price they see on the chart. In reality, the slippage from trying to enter a large position today instantly ate my profit margins. I spent the afternoon coding a realistic transaction cost estimator into the Python pipeline to stop my Sharpe ratio looking so delusional.

7th July 2026: Monte Carlo Stress TestsModel validation is brutal. I ran some tests today and realised that if a strategy prints money in the In-Sample data but completely tanks on the Out-of-Sample testing data, it is heavily overfitted. To combat this, I’m building a Rolling Train Validation setup. I also started looking into Monte Carlo simulations. Throwing thousands of random, mathematically generated variations at the model is the only way to find the absolute worst-case drawdown. It takes the gut feeling completely out of it.

8th July 2026: Hiding from HFTsToday's lecture on 'Implementation Shortfall' made me completely scrap my Week 1 execution logic. Previously, Gemini gave me a block of code that just dumped my entire capital allocation into the market via a single limit order. In a real institutional environment, high-frequency algorithms would see that massive order and front-run me instantly. I had to overwrite it and build a Smart Order Router (SOR) using a Time-Weighted Average Price (TWAP) algorithm to slice the orders up and hide my market impact.

10th July 2026: Dynamic Volatility StopsThe FTSE swings are getting ridiculous as the new government tries to sort out its fiscal policy. My arbitrary 2% stop-losses are getting hunted and whipsawed every single day. I completely ripped out the risk module today and coded Average True Range (ATR) dynamic stops instead. Now, the bot actually measures the daily volatility of the asset. If the market is calm, the stops tighten. If it’s chaotic, they widen out to 2.5x ATR to give the trade room to breathe.

13th July 2026: The Math of Volatility DragSpent hours working on portfolio mechanics and sizing. Volatility Drag is a harsh mathematical reality: taking a 50% loss requires a 100% gain just to get back to zero. To control the downside variance, I integrated a Fractional Kelly sizing model. It calculates the mathematically optimal bet size based on the win rate, but since backtests always degrade, I’m strictly running a "Half-Kelly" to suppress the risk. I also binned the Sharpe Ratio as my only metric. It unfairly penalises upside volatility, so I coded in the Sortino and Calmar ratios to properly measure returns against maximum drawdowns.

15th July 2026: Entering DerivativesMoving into derivatives this week felt like hitting a brick wall. Until now, my backtester was completely directional—buying assets and praying the stop-losses hold. But the relentless sideways chop showed me that overnight gap risks can easily blow past a stop-loss because of slippage. Black-Scholes clicked into place today. The fact that an option's premium is split between Intrinsic Value and Time Value completely flips how you manage risk. We coded the Newton-Raphson method for Implied Volatility (IV) to map the market's fear of fat-tail crashes.

18th July 2026: The Cost of InsuranceSpent the entire weekend trying to weave option Greeks into the backtesting architecture without breaking everything. The standard Black-Scholes formula assumes constant volatility, which is a total joke in the real world. Gemini kept trying to use a static risk-free rate in the code, which was throwing calculation errors everywhere. I had to step in and manually code a rolling historical volatility calculator so the bot could actually price synthetic option legs dynamically.I also integrated a Protective Put / Collar logic. By calculating the fractional cost of an Out-of-The-Money put option, the model mathematically accounts for the cost of insurance. My Calmar ratio dropped slightly because of the premium costs, but it proves institutional-grade safety isn't free.

22nd July 2026: Why MVO FailsToday shattered my reliance on standard Modern Portfolio Theory. I’ve been assuming that running a Momentum model next to a Mean Reversion model makes me naturally diversified. The reality is that total portfolio risk is driven by interaction. I looked into Mean-Variance Optimization (MVO), but quickly realised it’s a massive trap. During a simulated 2008-style crash, correlations will violently spike to 1.0, and MVO will just blow up the account. I binned it entirely and built a RobustPortfolioAllocator using an Inverse Volatility (Risk Parity) approach instead.

24th July 2026: Eigen DecompositionDived deeply into the linear algebra behind quantitative finance today. It is terrifying that multiple completely different trading strategies can actually be heavily exposed to the exact same hidden risk factor (like tech growth or cheap credit). I upgraded the risk architecture by coding an Eigen Decomposition matrix ($\Sigma = Q \Lambda Q^T$). It isolates the eigenvectors and eigenvalues so that if one factor explains more than 65% of the total portfolio variance, the bot throws a critical concentration warning.

26th July 2026: Final ChecksTomorrow the live 10-day simulation starts. Looking back over the last seven weeks, the architecture is finally solid. The pipeline fetches data, cleans it (handling splits and survivorship bias), runs it through diverse alpha generators like Stat-Arb and KAMA, and executes via the TWAP router. With the new Eigen risk allocator, the capital distribution is mathematically hardened. 26 years of market crashes are about to be compressed into 10 days. The system is ready. Let the stress test begin.

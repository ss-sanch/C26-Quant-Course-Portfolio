Day 2 of the live simulation was an absolute psychological meat grinder. The market regime was violently chopping sideways, meaning our Trend Following strategy was constantly getting whipsawed. Every time a sharp 5-minute price spike caused our fast 10-minute moving average to cross the 50-minute average, the bot executed a buy order, only for the price to instantly mean-revert. Thankfully, our tight, dynamic ATR (Average True Range) stop-losses did exactly what they were supposed to do, cutting the trades instantly to protect our capital from the noise.

However, we had a major architectural lag to fix. Our RobustPortfolioAllocator was calculating risk parity based on daily closing prices. Because the simulation had only just started, the allocator didn't have enough daily data to realise the market was a choppy mess, so it kept blindly feeding capital to the struggling trend models. I intervened and patched the allocator to calculate inverse volatility using a rolling 30-minute intraday lookback window instead. This worked brilliantly. When the bot tried to take another trade on Microsoft, the allocator recognised the high intraday variance and choked the position size down to roughly 10% of our portfolio, completely overriding the 35% hard cap. Because of this defensive sizing, despite taking roughly 10 consecutive losses over a three-hour window, our total drawdown was strictly contained to just 2.5%.

Pushing that code update highlighted another massive operational issue: state loss. Every time we restarted the Colab kernel to update the code, the bot's short-term RAM was wiped clean, forcing us to sit in cash for another 50 minutes while the moving averages gathered fresh data. To solve this, I built a MemoryManager module using Python's pickle library. Now, the bot silently saves its accumulated 200-minute price history to a local bot_memory.pkl file on the hard drive every 60 seconds. If the bot restarts, it instantly loads the file and bypasses the warm-up phase entirely
